| 2026-09-25 | On round trips, the walk-back return time is min(backtrack, continue forward around the loop). | The first version charged a full reverse loop at the end, a false violation (found on real terrain). |

| 2026-09-25 | Cesium on an exact sphere needs two narrow patches: `Ellipsoid.prototype.getSurfaceNormalIntersectionWithZAxis` returns "none" for buffered height rays on spheres (Cesium then uses its own 11.5 km-below-surface fallback), plus a try/catch around `Globe.getHeight`. | On a sphere the surface normal meets the z-axis at the body centre. That degenerate ray origin broke camera-height lookups: the render loop stopped and the camera could not go below the reference sphere (~2.5 km above the Jezero floor). The patch lives on the prototype because Cesium clones ellipsoids into web workers. |
| 2026-09-25 | Terrain tiles map pydelatin's y axis from the **south** edge. | pydelatin counts y from the bottom row. The first build mirrored every tile north–south (ledges at tile rows). A regression test pins orientation and winding. |
| 2026-09-25 | Space background: a transparent Cesium canvas over our own WebGL2 sky layer. Stars from BSC5 (V ≤ 6.5), Milky Way from NASA SVS Deep Star Maps 2020, Sun and planets from astronomy-engine, all rotated with the IAU 2015 Mars model at the planned EVA time. | Cesium's SkyBox is tied to Earth's rotation. Positions are exact; brightness is enhanced relative to a real exposure (stated in the layer's provenance). The sky projection matches Cesium's own projection to 0.1 px. |
| 2026-09-25 | The daytime surface sky tint is illustrative, and stars fade in daylight near the surface. | A real exposure would not show stars in the Martian daytime sky. No atmospheric scattering model is claimed. |
| 2026-09-25 | Controlled exploration volume: close flight only within ~32 km of Jezero (≥ 220 m above terrain). The altitude floor rises smoothly to 160 km by 450 km away; max altitude 24,000 km; free-look disabled. Scripted flights suspend the limits. | Keeps users in areas with detailed data without hard walls. The funnel is monotone (unit test), so there is no gap to slip through. Verified in the browser: Tharsis at 20 km pushed to 160 km; 30,000 km capped; below the Jezero floor pushed back to 220 m above terrain. |
| 2026-09-25 | Labels are DOM annotations with per-frame decluttering (priority, 4 candidate positions, planet occlusion, dedupe of a POI used as a waypoint) instead of Cesium labels. | Guarantees no overlapping text (verified: 0 overlaps among visible labels) and crisp typography. |
| 2026-09-25 | Routes are drawn from the exact computed geometry, densified every 12 m and draped on the rendered terrain; no corner smoothing. The primary route stays visible through terrain (glow). | Cosmetic smoothing could cut into blocked terrain. Densifying removes floating chords while keeping the scientific path. |
| 2026-09-25 | The Perseverance traverse is drawn as one sol-ordered line, simplified to 2.5 m for display (not 556 overlapping drive segments). Surface lines and markers are hidden beyond 300/450 km from the camera; from orbit a "Jezero Crater" label stands in. | Visual clarity at every scale. |
